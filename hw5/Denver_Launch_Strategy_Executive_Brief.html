What We Built, and What It Revealed
We started with a workflow problem: Marketing metrics were manual—spreadsheets, Friday afternoon reporting, no automation. That's inefficient. But when we actually analyzed what those metrics meant, we found something bigger: Austin's market and Denver's market are fundamentally different.
So we looked at Austin's channel performance expecting to copy it. And that's where the insight landed: Email and Instagram don't do the same job. Instagram is lead generation—high ROAS ($6.20 per $1), fast conversion, mostly single purchases. Email is retention—lower per-dollar return ($4.00 ROAS), but customers who bought on Email spend $693 on average over time (92% repeat rate). Completely different muscles.
Then we tested the obvious next step: apply Austin's channel mix to Denver. The custom assistant ran the scenario and it failed. Austin's ratios don't predict Denver demand because the season changes everything. November isn't May. Premium gift-buyers aren't summer-casual shoppers. The data was clear: we'd been planning a spring launch strategy for a winter market.
That's why Hoodie hero. It's not just a product choice—it's the channel insight (Instagram for awareness of premium product) + the market insight (Denver November is scarcity + gift-giving, not volume + price) coming together. The Shorts miss becomes a feature, not a bug: "Coming mid-November" positions exclusivity instead of covering a gap.
